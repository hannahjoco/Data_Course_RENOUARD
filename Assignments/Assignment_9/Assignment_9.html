The dataset contains 400 observations and 4 variables: admission outcome, GRE score, GPA, and undergraduate institution rank. Because admit is binary, I will use a logistic regression model.

Next, I will explore how admission varies with undergraduate institution rank.

ggplot(admissions, aes(x = rank, fill = factor(admit))) +
  geom_bar(position = "fill") +
  labs(x = "Undergraduate institution rank",
       y = "Proportion",
       fill = "Admit")

This plot suggests that students from higher-ranked institutions have a higher proportion of admission compared to those from lower-ranked institutions.

I will also compare GRE score distributions between admitted and non-admitted students.

ggplot(admissions, aes(x = gre, fill = factor(admit))) +
  geom_density(alpha = 0.4) +
  labs(x = "GRE score",
       fill = "Admit")

This plot suggests that admitted students tend to have higher GRE scores than students who weren’t admitted.

Now I will compare GPA distributions between admitted and non-admitted students.

ggplot(admissions, aes(x = gpa, fill = factor(admit))) +
  geom_density(alpha = 0.4) +
  labs(x = "GPA",
       fill = "Admit")

This plot suggests that admitted students tend to have higher GPAs than non-admitted students.

Based on these exploratory plots, GRE score, GPA, and undergraduate institution rank all appear to influence admission, so I will fit several logistic regression models.

mod_gpa <- glm(admit ~ gpa, data = admissions, family = binomial)
mod_gre_gpa <- glm(admit ~ gre + gpa, data = admissions, family = binomial)
model_full <- glm(admit ~ gre + gpa + rank, data = admissions, family = binomial)

I will compare these models using AIC, where lower values indicate a better balance of model fit and complexity.

AIC(mod_gpa, mod_gre_gpa, model_full)
##             df      AIC
## mod_gpa      2 490.9676
## mod_gre_gpa  3 486.3440
## model_full   6 470.5175

The full model had the lowest AIC value, indicating that including GRE score, GPA, and undergraduate institution rank provides the best fit among the models considered.

The full model showed that GRE score and GPA were both significant positive predictors of admission. This indicates that students with higher GRE scores and higher GPAs had greater odds of being admitted.

Undergraduate institution rank was also an important predictor. Compared with students from rank 1 institutions, students from ranks 2, 3, and 4 had lower odds of admission. The negative coefficients for these levels indicate that lower-ranked institutions were associated with reduced admission odds.

Overall, the results suggest that both academic performance and undergraduate institution rank contribute meaningfully to graduate school admission outcomes.

Conclusion

This analysis used logistic regression to model graduate school admission based on GRE score, GPA, and undergraduate institution rank. The results indicate that higher GRE scores, higher GPAs, and attending a higher-ranked undergraduate institution are all associated with a greater likelihood of admission.
